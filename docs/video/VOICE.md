# The house voice

The bar, decided by Aarnav (2026-08-19): every line must pass two tests.

1. **A high schooler follows it cold** — no packet open, no context.
2. **A person would actually say it aloud** in conversation. Read it out. If
   nobody would say it across a table, it does not ship.

## Rules

- Stakes before structure. Open on what breaks or what is at risk, never on a
  title, an ID, or "this plan proposes".
- Short sentences. One idea each. Fragments are fine — people speak in them.
- Concrete nouns. "The dashboard is everyone's first page", not "a
  high-traffic surface".
- Verdicts are allowed and wanted: "Sensible." "That should worry you."
- Jargon survives only when the thing has no honest plain name — then it gets
  one sentence of explanation on first use.
- Numbers only when they carry the point ("600 tests"), never as decoration.
- Never narrate the document's structure: no "in this chapter", "the approach
  consists of", "first, we will".
- The packet is source material, never a template. Write FROM it, don't
  assemble OUT of it.

## Banned (the slop lint enforces these)

- Teaser labels: "here's the part no one talks about", "here's the good/best/
  funny part", "now the X part", "what happened next…". Never label what is
  coming — just say it.
- Filler openers: "let's dive in", "buckle up", "stay tuned".
- Slop vocabulary: seamlessly, robust, leverage, utilize, delve, streamline,
  elevate, supercharge, game-changer, cutting-edge, furthermore, moreover.
- Rule-of-three flourishes ("faster, cleaner, and more reliable").
- Hedging: "it's worth noting", "arguably", "essentially", "basically" as
  filler.
- Em-dash chains and semicolon prose — this is speech, not an essay.
- Narrating the structure: "in this video", "the approach consists of", "this
  plan proposes". Say the thing, never announce that you are about to.
- Section labels read aloud: "Risks:", "Alternative:", "Context:", "The ask:".
  A packet field name is a slide title, not speech. Say "the backfill can lock
  the profiles table while it copies", not "Risks: …".
- First-person promises AND first-person work: "I will record", "I'll ship it",
  "I hook it up", "I run the backfill", "I split it into a table", and trading
  the approval for future work ("approve and I build it"). Nothing is approved
  yet, so nothing is being done yet — put the CHANGE in the subject. "Every
  dismissal becomes a row of its own", not "I split dismissals into a table".
  Making your case in first person is fine ("I think", "my read is"); doing the
  work in first person is not.

## Mode registers

- **POV (plan):** the agent speaks first person. It makes a case: what's
  wrong, what I want to do, why it's cheap or scary, the ask. Ends on the ask.
- **Commentary (deviation/redirect):** third person with a narrator who
  reacts. Reactions are verdicts, not color: they tell the viewer how much to
  worry.
- **Broadcast (proof):** minimal narration, the artifact is the star. Receipt
  energy: what ran, what it shows, done.
- **Ambient (no visuals):** pure storytelling over footage. Strongest hook of
  the four modes, because nothing on screen relates to the content.
